Build a scenario‑based forecast that translates your latest evidence (Measure + tests) into a profit‑aligned plan, then export a weekly pacing worksheet to operationalize it.

<br />

Audience: CMO/VP Growth, CFO/FP&A, Performance & Media, Analytics & Insights
Goal: Choose a plan (efficiency or growth) with clear trade‑offs and guardrails, then hand off a paced budget by channel/week
Time: 45–90 minutes (assuming a recent Measure read and data is connected)
Prerequisites: “Connect your data” and “Run your first Measure read”; optional recent geo‑lift tests for key channels
Outcomes: 2–3 forecasted scenarios, a chosen plan with uncertainty ranges, and an exportable pacing worksheet + guardrails

<br />

At a glance

<br />

You’ll get: Expected outcomes (revenue, profit, CAC/payback), spend by channel/week, uncertainty bands, and recommended caps/floors.

<br />

Start with 3 scenarios: Efficiency‑first, Balanced, Growth‑first.

<br />

Pick one objective: Profit, Revenue, or CAC/Payback—and keep it consistent across scenarios.

<br />

Use constraints: Channel floors/ceilings, ramp rates, geo mix, promo/retail windows, and business rules (e.g., cap retargeting).

<br />

Step 1 — Set the planning objective

<br />

Choose one objective for this cycle and write it down so every scenario uses the same yardstick:

<br />

Maximize Profit (default for most brands)

<br />

Maximize Revenue (when gaining share or hitting top‑line targets)

<br />

Hit a CAC or Payback target (when cash cycle or LTV thresholds matter most)

<br />

Add a secondary guardrail (e.g., “Payback ≤ 3 months” or “ROAS ≥ 2.0”) to avoid extreme solutions.

<br />

Step 2 — Define the forecast context

<br />

Horizon: 4–12 weeks is typical for a planning cycle; extend if you have seasonal peaks.

<br />

Base period: Choose a recent, representative window as the baseline for comparisons.

<br />

Budget envelope: Set total spend (or a range) the optimizer must respect.

<br />

Seasonality & promos: Load known events (sales, launches, holidays) that will shift demand or supply.

<br />

Step 3 — Load evidence and assumptions

<br />

Select your latest Measure run (incremental contribution + marginal returns curves).

<br />

Ensure any geo‑test lift has been applied to calibrate tactics/channels.

<br />

Confirm price/promo calendars, retail/POS signals, and major campaign plans (e.g., new creative drop).

<br />

If something material changed since the last Measure run, note the assumption and plan a follow‑up test.

<br />

Step 4 — Add constraints & guardrails

<br />

Model reality so the plan is executable:

<br />

Floors/ceilings by channel/tactic: e.g., minimum presence on brand channels; cap Retargeting or Brand Search.

<br />

Ramp rates: Limit week‑over‑week changes to respect learning and operational capacity.

<br />

Geo mix or retailer windows: Lock spend where contractual or shelf constraints apply.

<br />

Inventory & supply constraints: Avoid planning spend on SKUs with stock risk.

<br />

Risk guardrails: Minimum ROAS / maximum CAC / maximum share in any single channel.

<br />

Business rules: Respect creative refresh cadence and known media lead times (CTV, print).

<br />

Step 5 — Build 2–3 scenarios

<br />

Create distinct, defensible options:

<br />

Efficiency‑first

<br />

Tight guardrails, lower ceilings on lower‑funnel harvesters, favor steep marginal curves.

<br />

Balanced

<br />

Moderate scaling on proven upper‑funnel; maintain brand presence; reasonable caps on harvesters.

<br />

Growth‑first

<br />

Higher budget envelope and relaxed caps; scale channels with positive lift and headroom; accept lower near‑term efficiency.

<br />

Keep the same objective across scenarios, changing only budgets and constraints.

<br />

Step 6 — Run the forecast and read the outputs

<br />

For each scenario, review:

<br />

Expected outcomes: Profit/revenue/CAC (per your objective), with uncertainty bands.

<br />

Spend plan: Channel/week allocations with notes on ramps and ceilings hit.

<br />

Top moves: Where budget shifts and why (based on marginal curves and lift).

<br />

Risk notes: Channels near saturation, weeks with high variance, or assumptions driving the result.

<br />

Sanity checks: Compare results to the base period, prior cycles, and known seasonality; nothing should defy common sense.

<br />

Step 7 — Compare scenarios & choose a plan

<br />

Use a simple decision frame:

<br />

Objective score: Which scenario wins on the chosen objective?

<br />

Downside risk: Which has tighter uncertainty bands and fewer “red flag” weeks?

<br />

Feasibility: Can teams execute the ramps, creative needs, and geo mix?

<br />

Finance view: Does profit/payback align with P&L targets and cash needs?

<br />

Pick one scenario as “Approved Plan – Quarter/Month” and note why (one‑liner decision rationale).

<br />

Step 8 — Export pacing & hand off to channels

<br />

Export: Download the pacing worksheet (channel x week spend, ramps, and notes).

<br />

Guardrails: Include caps/floors, target KPIs (incrementality‑adjusted), and alerts for variance thresholds.

<br />

Assignments: Channel owners accept the plan and confirm trafficking feasibility and timelines.

<br />

Change control: Establish how deviations are approved and logged.

<br />

Step 9 — Set monitoring & re‑plan cadence

<br />

Weekly: Realized vs forecast by channel/week; explain drivers of variance early.

<br />

Monthly: Minor re‑plans if variance persists or evidence shifts.

<br />

Quarterly: Full refresh after new Measure run and major test read‑outs.

<br />

Document triggers for off‑cycle re‑plans (e.g., supply shock, platform outage, policy change).

<br />

Definition of done

<br />

2–3 scenarios created with a consistent objective and documented constraints

<br />

One scenario approved with decision rationale and risk notes

<br />

Pacing worksheet exported and handed off to channel owners

<br />

Guardrails and monitoring cadence defined; change control documented

<br />

Common pitfalls (and quick fixes)

<br />

Optimizing to average ROAS instead of marginal returns
Fix: Use marginal curves from Measure; cap channels at saturation points.

<br />

Ignoring promo/seasonality
Fix: Load calendar events; adjust expectations and guardrails for peak weeks.

<br />

Unrealistic ramps
Fix: Add ramp‑rate constraints and creative/ops capacity checks.

<br />

Over‑reliance on lower‑funnel
Fix: Set ceilings on Retargeting/Brand Search; re‑invest in proven prospecting and upper‑funnel with positive lift.

<br />

No uncertainty view
Fix: Always review bands; pick scenarios with acceptable downside risk, not just best medians.

<br />

Templates & artifacts

<br />

Scenario brief (objective, base period, budget envelope, constraints, guardrails)

<br />

Scenario comparison sheet (objective score, risk notes, key moves)

<br />

Pacing worksheet (channel x week spend + caps/floors + ramp notes)

<br />

Change log (date, owner, reason, approved deviation)

<br />

What happens next

<br />

Enforce in Optimize: Apply caps, floors, and alerts; align daily bidding to incrementality‑adjusted KPIs.

<br />

Queue experiments: Add geo tests for high‑spend areas with uncertainty; schedule creative/audience pilots.

<br />

Close the loop: Feed realized results back into Measure; refresh scenarios next cycle.

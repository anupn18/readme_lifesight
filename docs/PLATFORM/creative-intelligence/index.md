## What Creative Intelligence does

Most platforms tell you how a creative performed. Creative Intelligence goes a step further and tells you what that performance means and what to do next.

For every creative, it answers three questions:

1. How is it doing? It scores the creative against comparable creatives and groups it into a clear state.
2. Why? It gives a short, specific read that references the creative's own numbers.
3. What should you do? It suggests a next step, such as scale it, refresh it, or pause it.

You review one channel at a time, and creatives are never compared across channels. A creative also needs a little spend and time live before it can be confidently classified. Until then it is left unclassified rather than judged on thin data.

## How creatives are compared

A text ad and a video ad are not the same thing, so judging them by the same numbers would be misleading.

Creative Intelligence compares every creative only against others of the same asset type: text with text, image with image, and video with video. A creative's standing is always relative to comparable peers, which is what makes the read fair and useful.

## Scores: how a creative ranks

Two scores tell you where a creative stands among its peers. Both run from 0 to 100 and are percentile ranks, so a score of 80 means the creative does better than 80% of others of the same asset type.

**Click score&#x20;**&#x72;anks the creative by click-through rate (CTR). A high click score means people click it more often than comparable creatives.
**Conversion score** ranks the creative by conversion rate (CVR). A high conversion score means the clicks it earns are more likely to convert.

Reading the two together is the core skill. It helps to picture them as a grid:

## The Four States

![](https://files.readme.io/6532ed403f638cbdfb81f13857d35c73411e6df228ee6ad4a2995234e10e508d-Creative-Intelligence-signal-strip.png)

<br />

Your creatives are grouped into four states so you can see where to focus.

- **Winners** are strong on both clicks and conversions, with enough spend and time live to be sure. These are your proven creatives and candidates to put more budget behind.
- **Fatiguing creatives&#x20;**&#x61;re declining as the audience tires of them. Catch these early and refresh before results drop further.
- **Losers&#x20;**&#x61;re weak on both clicks and conversions, with enough spend and time live to be sure. They are candidates to pause so the budget can go elsewhere.
- **New creatives&#x20;**&#x77;ere recently launched and are still gathering data. They are worth watching, as early signals appear here before a creative is proven.

Winners and Losers only include creatives that have run with enough spend and for enough days to judge them fairly. A promising or poor creative with very little data stays out of these states until there is enough evidence.

## Fatigue: catching decline early

Fatigue happens when an audience has seen a creative so often that it stops responding. Creative Intelligence watches each creative's click-through rate over its recent history and flags the trend:

Healthy. Click-through rate is steady or improving.
Fatiguing. Click-through rate has started to fall. Plan a refresh.
Exhausted. Click-through rate has fallen sharply. Refresh or replace the creative.

Days with very low impressions are set aside before the trend is measured, so a single click on a quiet day cannot look like a spike or a crash. Fatigue is only flagged when there is enough recent data to trust the trend.

## Signals: your one-line diagnosis

Every creative is given a single signal: the most important thing about it right now, with a short read and a suggested action.

📘 What the signal colors mean

Green means something is working well. Amber means it is worth attention soon. Red means it needs attention now. Grey means there is not enough to say yet.

Signals fall into four themes.

### **Lifecycle: where the creative is in its life**

- **Early winner.&#x20;**&#x41; new creative that is already performing well. A candidate to scale, even though it is young.
- **Weak launch.** A new creative that is underperforming out of the gate. Watch it before committing more budget.
- **Fatigue onset.&#x20;**&#x43;lick-through rate is beginning to slip. Plan a refresh before it worsens.
- **Exhausted.&#x20;**&#x43;lick-through rate has dropped sharply and the audience has seen it too often. Refresh or replace it.
- **Zombie.** Still spending but barely being shown, and no longer new. Review delivery or pause it.

### Spend efficiency: what you get for the money

- **Scale opportunity.** Performs well on your primary KPI but is under-spent. There is room to put more budget behind it.
- **Budget drain.** Takes meaningful spend but returns poorly. A candidate to pause and reallocate.
- **Cost efficient**. Lower than typical on both cost per click and cost per thousand impressions. Efficient reach, and good to keep running.

### Funnel: clicks versus conversions

- **Full-funnel winner.&#x20;**&#x53;trong on both clicks and conversions. One of your best all-rounders. Use it as a benchmark.
- **Hook-convert gap**. Earns clicks but does not convert as well. The hook works, so check the offer or landing page.
- **Hidden gem.&#x20;**&#x44;oes not earn many clicks but converts strongly when it does. It may be under-shown and worth more exposure.

### Video: how people watch

These signals apply to video creatives, based on how much of the video people watch.

High retention. People watch a large share of the video. Strong, engaging video and a candidate to scale.
Drop-off cliff. People start watching but leave quickly. The opening works, but the middle loses them. Tighten it.
Low attention. People barely watch. The opening is not landing, so rework the first few seconds.

**When there is not enough to say**

If none of the above clearly applies, a creative is marked Under review. This is neutral. It usually means the creative is too new or too low-volume to read confidently, and it will be classified once more data arrives.

## The metrics on a card

Each creative card shows the numbers behind its scores and signal.

![](https://files.readme.io/c86c2f1ed42f789ea79b2d1b33ea0cf5c5b69a7d4ec814e55c18015b6e67b3ca-Creative-Intelligence-dashboard.png)

<br />

- **CTR (click-through rate).** How often people click after seeing the creative. Higher means more compelling.
- **CVR (conversion rate).&#x20;**&#x48;ow often a click leads to a conversion. Higher means the clicks are well qualified.
- **CPC (cost per click).&#x20;**&#x57;hat you pay, on average, for each click.
- **CPM (cost per thousand impressions).&#x20;**&#x57;hat you pay to be seen a thousand times.
- **ROAS (return on ad spend).&#x20;**&#x52;evenue earned per unit of spend. Higher is better.
- **CPA (cost per acquisition).&#x20;**&#x57;hat you pay for each conversion. Lower is better.

A card shows either ROAS or CPA as its headline number, depending on your account's primary KPI.

## How to read a creative card

Each creative appears as a card with two sides.

The front shows performance: the thumbnail, the creative's name, its headline number (ROAS or CPA), spend, and the core metrics, with badges flagging a new or fatiguing creative at a glance.

The back shows the diagnosis: the signal and its color, the one-line read, the suggested action, a small chart of recent click-through rate against the benchmark for that asset type, and, for video, the watch metrics.

In short, the front tells you how the creative is doing, and the back tells you what to do about it.

**A worked example**

To see how the pieces fit together, take one text creative from the last 30 days, on an account whose primary KPI is ROAS. Its numbers are:

**Spend:** $5,72&#x39;**&#xA;Impressions:&#x20;**&#x32;0,829 and Clicks: 8,73&#x32;**&#xA;CTR:&#x20;**&#x34;1.9% and ROAS: 6.7&#x78;**&#xA;Click score:&#x20;**&#x37;4 and Conversion score: 8&#x32;**&#xA;Fatigue:** healthy

Here is how Creative Intelligence reads it:

Fair comparison. It is a text creative, so it is ranked against your other text creatives, not your images or videos.
Scores. A click score of 74 means it earns clicks more often than 74% of your other text creatives.

&#x20;A conversion score of 82 means its clicks convert better than 82% of them. Strong on both.
State. With both scores well above the line, and enough spend and days behind it, it lands in Winners.
Signal. Because it is strong on both clicks and conversions, the diagnosis is Full-funnel winner (green).&#x20;

The card reads: "Click score 74 and conversion score 82. CTR of 41.9% and CVR of 7.55% are both in the top quartile for text creatives," with the action "Increase daily budget by 20% and test this copy in additional ad groups to validate scale."

**What you would do. Scale it, and use it as a benchmark for new creative briefs.**

Now take a different creative: a prescription-glasses text ad spending $2,229, with a CTR of 21.6% and ROAS of 3.5x.&#x20;

Those are healthy numbers on their own. But its daily click-through rate has been sliding over the last two weeks, so its fatigue status is Fatiguing and its signal becomes Fatigue onset (amber): "CTR is declining and is currently 21.6%.&#x20;

The last 7-day average is below peak performance," with the action "Schedule a variant test now. Allocate 20% of your last 30-day spend to a refreshed version before CTR drops further."

**🚧 Snapshot versus trend**

The second creative's headline numbers still look fine. The trend is what flips the signal. That is why a creative's signal can change while its numbers still look good, and why catching fatigue early protects budget.

## Setting your primary KPI

Creative Intelligence adapts to what matters most for your business. Your account is set to one primary KPI, either ROAS for revenue-focused accounts or CPA for lead-focused accounts.

This setting changes the headline number on every card and in the summary, and it shapes the signals. For ROAS, higher is better. For CPA, lower is better. The rest of the read works the same way regardless.

**A simple weekly routine**

A simple weekly rhythm gets the most from Creative Intelligence:

- Scale your winners.
- Look at Winners and Scale opportunities, and shift budget toward what is proven.
- Refresh what is fatiguing.&#x20;
- Act on Fatigue onset and Exhausted creatives before results fall further.Cut the drains.&#x20;
- Pause Losers and Budget drains, and move that spend to better performers.
- Watch the new ones. Keep an eye on New creatives and Early winners to spot your next winners sooner.

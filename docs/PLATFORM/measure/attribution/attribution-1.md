Marketing attribution helps you understand which marketing efforts are driving conversions. By implementing a solid attribution strategy, you can accurately measure campaign effectiveness, optimize your spend, and prove the business impact of your activities.

This guide explores two primary approaches to attribution available within the Lifesight UMM Platform: traditional **Touch-Based Attribution** and the more advanced **Causal Attribution** offered by our Unified Measurement framework.

### Understanding Touch-Based Attribution

Touch-based attribution, often referred to as Multi-Touch Attribution (MTA), works by assigning credit for a conversion to the various digital touchpoints (like clicks and impressions) that a user interacted with before converting. It provides insights at the user level, helping you understand the digital customer journey.

There are several common models for distributing this credit:

* **First-Touch:** Assigns 100% of conversion credit to the first touchpoint.
* **Last-Touch:** Assigns 100% of conversion credit to the final touchpoint.
* **Linear:** Divides credit equally across all touchpoints.
* **Time-Decay:** Gives more credit to touchpoints closer to the conversion.
* **U-Shaped:** Assigns 40% credit to the first touch and 40% to the last, distributing the remaining 20% to the middle touches.

<Callout icon="ℹ️" theme="info">
  **When to Use Touch-Based Models**

  Touch-based attribution is powerful for gaining a deeper understanding of digital touchpoints and optimizing online campaigns in near real-time. However, it has limitations. It often ignores offline activities and external factors, resulting in an incomplete view of your total marketing impact.
</Callout>

### Causal Attribution: The Unified Measurement Approach

While touch-based models show which channels were present on the conversion path, Causal Attribution goes a step further. It moves beyond correlation to determine the true, incremental impact of each marketing activity on your business goals.

This is not a single model, but rather a **Unified Marketing Measurement (UMM)** methodology that maximizes the use of all available data to create a single source of truth. It achieves this by blending the strengths of different techniques:

* **Marketing Mix Modeling (MMM)**: Provides a holistic, top-down view of performance across all channels, including offline media and external factors.
* **Experiments**: Generate precise insights into the incrementality of specific campaigns or ads.
* **Incrementality-calibrated Forecasting**: Budget recommendations to reach Revenue or other KPI goals which are rooted in incremental contributions of channels in the marketing mix

By blending these methodologies, causal attribution can measure the cross-effects between online and offline channels. For example, it can determine how a TV commercial influenced searches and direct website visits, providing a complete and unbiased view of performance.

<Callout icon="👍" theme="okay">
  **Advantages of a Unified Approach**

  * **Holistic**: Measures marketing effectiveness across all channels and includes non-marketing factors.
  * **Accurate**: Leads to more precise measurement, improved budget allocations, and increased revenue.
  * **Flexible**: Handles data with different levels of granularity from various sources.
  * **Reliable**: Uses advanced statistics to get a grip on unreliable results and correct them to obtain more accurate outcomes.
</Callout>

| Feature               | Touch-Based Attribution                                                                               | Causal Attribution                                                                             |
| :-------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Core Logic**        | Distributes credit across user touchpoints based on a predefined rule (e.g., last-click).             | Measures the incremental business impact by blending multiple data sources and models.         |
| **Channels Measured** | Primarily online channels (e.g., Search, Social, Display).                                            | All online and offline channels (e.g., TV, Radio, Print) plus external factors.                |
| **Data Granularity**  | User-level (clicks, impressions).                                                                     | Blends user-level data with aggregated data (e.g., spend, GRPs) for a complete view.           |
| **Key Benefit**       | Good for near real-time, tactical optimization of online channels.                                    | Provides a single source of truth for strategic, cross-channel budget allocation and planning. |
| **Key Limitation**    | **Ignores offline media** and external factors, leading to an incomplete and potentially biased view. | **Requires comprehensive data** and a more advanced setup to implement effectively.            |

<br />

### Which Model Should You Use?

Choosing the right attribution approach depends on your specific goals and the complexity of your marketing efforts.

* Choose ***Touch-Based Attribution*** for tactical, real-time optimization of your digital channels.
* Choose ***Causal Attribution*** for strategic budget allocation and to get a complete, accurate view of your total marketing ROI across both online and offline channels.

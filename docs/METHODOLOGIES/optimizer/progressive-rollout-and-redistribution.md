The Optimizer doesn’t just recommend changes — it makes sure those changes are applied in a way that campaigns can actually absorb. This means budgets are rolled out **progressively** and, when needed, **redistributed** to maximize efficiency.

### Why Progressive Rollout Matters

Sudden, large budget increases can:

* Disrupt campaign learning phases
* Cause inefficient spend spikes
* Lead to overspending before platforms can adapt

To prevent this, the Optimizer applies increases gradually, respecting pacing limits and campaign health. This ensures every recommendation is rolled out **smoothly and sustainably**.

> 📘 _Example:_
>
> * Instead of instantly doubling a campaign’s budget, the Optimizer may stage the increase over several days, allowing the campaign to adjust and continue performing effectively.

### Redistribution of Unused Budget

Sometimes, not every campaign can fully spend its recommended budget. This can happen if:

* The campaign is near its end date
* Daily caps or lifetime caps are already being hit
* The campaign isn’t delivering enough conversions to justify more spend

When this occurs, the Optimizer **redistributes unused budget** to other eligible campaigns in the same plan.

This way, no budget goes to waste — it gets redirected to campaigns that can make the best use of it.

### What You’ll See in the Optimizer

* Recommendations that seem **smaller than expected** → These often reflect pacing limits or campaign caps.
* Other campaigns receiving **higher increases than expected** → This reflects redistribution from campaigns that couldn’t absorb their allocation.
* Inline warnings (⚠️) → Highlight why some campaigns may not be scaled (e.g., “Ends in 3 days,” “Low conversions”).

### Why This Matters

* Guarantees **safe scaling** → budgets grow at a pace campaigns can handle.
* Ensures **no wasted spend** → unallocated dollars get redirected automatically.
* Gives **confidence** → recommendations aren’t just optimal on paper, they’re practical for execution.

Aligning marketing and finance means replacing competing dashboards with a single, causal truth and operating to a shared plan—where budgets, forecasts, and daily KPIs reconcile to revenue, margin, and payback, with transparent uncertainty and clear decision rights.

At a glance

Use when: Platform numbers conflict with finance; re‑plans stall; growth vs. profitability debates repeat; “whose numbers are right?” is a weekly meeting.

You’ll achieve: One measurement foundation, a finance‑ready KPI framework (iROAS/iCAC, payback, profit), a risk‑aware planning process, and a governance cadence that speeds approvals.

Core tools: Causal MMM · Geo‑based Incrementality Testing · Incrementality‑calibrated Attribution · Ensemble Forecasting · Scenario Planner & Guardrails · Executive Dashboards.

Audience, goal, and outcomes

Audience: CMO/VP Growth, CFO & FP&A, Performance & Media, Analytics & Insights, Agency Partners
Goal: Make faster, higher‑confidence budget decisions by reconciling marketing performance to P&L outcomes.
Timeframe: 4–8 weeks for full alignment (quick wins in the first 1–2 weeks).
Outcomes:

Signed Measurement & Planning Charter (who decides what, using which metrics, how often)

Shared KPI dictionary and reporting views (raw vs incrementality‑adjusted vs P&L)

Calibrated daily metrics and an approved, risk‑aware plan with guardrails

Governance: weekly run‑of‑business, monthly calibration sweep, quarterly business review (QBR)

What “alignment” actually means (clear definitions)

Single source of decision truth: Marketing and finance operate from one reconciled view: incremental outcomes and profit/payback that trace back to actuals.

Causal foundation: MMM and geo tests set the truth; attribution is calibrated to match it for daily control.

Risk‑aware planning: Scenarios are chosen on profit, revenue, or payback with p50/p10/p90 ranges—no false precision.

Operational guardrails: Caps/floors and ramp limits keep daily optimization inside finance‑approved bounds.

Documented decision rights: Everyone knows who approves experiments, re‑plans, and guardrail changes.

Step‑by‑step plan

1. Draft the Measurement & Planning Charter (Days 1–5)

Create a 2‑page working agreement signed by CMO + CFO:

Business questions & decisions: What we must decide monthly/quarterly (mix shifts, re‑plans, test approvals).

Primary KPIs: Incremental Revenue/Orders/Subscriptions; iROAS/iCAC; Contribution Profit; Payback window.

Data & method policy: MMM + geo tests as truth; attribution calibrated; total‑to‑truth dedupe; standardized lookbacks.

Cadence: Weekly ops, monthly calibration, quarterly refresh/QBR.

RACI: Who proposes, who decides, who implements, who audits.

2. Establish reconciled baselines (Days 3–10)

Measurement: Run Measure to decompose baseline vs incremental and generate marginal returns; publish raw vs incrementality‑adjusted KPI views.

Finance mapping: Define variable margin, returns/COGS, taxes/fees, and revenue recognition rules that connect marketing metrics to contribution profit and payback.

Hygiene: Align currencies, time zones, fiscal calendars, and attribution windows.

3. Prove and calibrate (Weeks 2–6)

Geo tests: Run 1–2 high‑stakes tests (e.g., CTV scale‑up; Retargeting cap) to produce decision‑grade lift.

Calibration: Apply lift to MMM and calibrate attribution so daily KPIs match causal truth. Surface raw vs adjusted side‑by‑side.

4. Approve a risk‑aware plan (Week 3–6)

Scenarios: Build Efficiency, Balanced, and Growth options using Ensemble Forecasting.

Objective: Choose one (Profit, Revenue, or Payback) for this cycle.

Decision: Select the scenario that wins at p50 and remains acceptable at p10; record one‑line rationale.

5. Operationalize with guardrails (Week 4–6)

Caps/floors: Set ceilings on harvesters (Retargeting, Brand Search) and floors on proven prospecting (YouTube/CTV/Social).

Ramp rates: Limit WoW spend changes (e.g., ±20%).

Pacing export: Publish channel × week spend and target incrementality‑adjusted KPIs (iROAS, iCAC, payback).

Change control: Define who approves deviations and how they’re logged.

6. Run the cadence (ongoing)

Weekly Run‑of‑Business: Realized vs forecast, variances explained, no re‑litigating methods.

Monthly Calibration Sweep: Refresh adjustments after decisive tests; retire stale factors; minor re‑plans as needed.

Quarterly Business Review: MMM refresh, scenario comparison, cap/floor resets, and next test agenda.

The shared KPI framework (marketing ↔ finance)
Layer	Metric	Purpose	Owner
Operations	iROAS / iCAC (incrementality‑adjusted)	Daily optimization aligned to causal truth	Performance
Planning	mROAS curves & saturation	Allocate marginal dollars; set caps/floors	Growth/Analytics
Finance	Contribution Profit	Connect marketing to P&L; scenario approval	FP&A
Risk	Payback (p50/p10)	Resilience of plan under downside	CMO/CFO
Learning	Lift (geo tests)	Evidence to calibrate models & KPIs	Analytics

Standardize windows: e.g., 7‑day click / 1‑day view unless explicitly approved otherwise, and keep consistent across sources.

Reconciliation rules (non‑negotiables)

Total‑to‑truth: Adjusted conversions/revenue cannot exceed actuals.

Window alignment: Use the same lookbacks for comparison; otherwise fix hygiene before debating results.

Deduplication: Resolve overlap across platforms; consolidate Brand vs Non‑Brand and Prospecting vs Retargeting.

Revenue recognition: Agree how to treat returns, cancellations, and subscription churn.

Currency/timezone/fiscal: One standard per workspace; document exceptions.

Data requirements (good → better → best)

Good: Weekly outcomes (orders/revenue/subscriptions), channel spend, standardized windows, seasonality/promo calendar.

Better: Geo splits, POS/retail roll‑ups, price/promo intensity, creative/audience tags; incrementality‑adjusted daily KPIs.

Best: Recent geo test results, latest MMM curves, inventory/distribution constraints, macro/competitive signals.

Guardrails library (alignment‑friendly defaults)

Retargeting cap: ≤ X% of paid conversions after calibration.

Brand Search guardrail: Exact‑match priority; impression share cap; negatives for cannibalization.

Prospecting floor: ≥ Y% of paid spend in reach‑building channels with proven lift.

Ramp‑rate limit: ±20% WoW per channel; exceptions require CFO‑acknowledged change log.

Channel concentration: ≤40% in any single channel without C‑suite sign‑off.

Re‑plan triggers: Breach of p10 band for 2 consecutive weeks, major supply shock, policy change, or creative overhaul.

What “good” looks like after one cycle

Single causal truth accepted by marketing and finance; fewer “numbers” debates.

Plan approval time drops from weeks to days; re‑plans use the same rules.

Variance within bands for most weeks; outliers explained and logged.

Budget mix shifts toward channels with proven lift; caps enforced on harvesters.

Evidence cadence: 1–2 decisive geo tests per quarter; calibration updates on schedule.

Metrics that prove alignment improved

Forecast coverage: % weeks actuals within p10–p90 bands (goal ≥ 70–80%).

Approval speed: Median time to approve plans/re‑plans.

Discrepancy delta: Gap between platform totals and calibrated totals (trend ↓).

Efficiency with growth: iCAC/Payback on plan and NTB growth maintained or rising.

Ticket volume: “Whose numbers are right?” requests ↓ quarter‑over‑quarter.

Common pitfalls (and how to avoid them)

Debating methods, not decisions → Lock the Charter; route disputes to experiments and schedule a re‑test.

Point estimates only → Always publish p50/p10/p90 and choose plans with acceptable downside.

Taxonomy/window drift → Enforce naming standards and lookbacks; don’t calibrate on broken inputs.

Set‑and‑forget calibration → Refresh after decisive tests; retire stale factors.

Caps without floors → Starving prospecting hurts future demand; maintain floors where lift is proven.

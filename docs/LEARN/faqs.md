## What does Lifesight do?

Lifesight is an agentic Unified Marketing Measurement (UMM) platform that helps consumer brands grow by measuring what their marketing incrementally drives, not what ad platforms claim. It runs causal Marketing Mix Modeling (MMM), incrementality testing, and causal attribution as one orchestrated system in its Causal AI layer, with an Agentic AI layer on top that turns every result into answers, recommendations, and next steps.

## What is unified marketing measurement?

Unified marketing measurement combines multiple measurement methods, such as MMM, incrementality testing, and causal attribution, so each one calibrates the others. Instead of three tools giving three different answers, you get one causal view of marketing's incremental contribution to business growth, from quarterly planning down to daily optimization.

## Who is Lifesight for?

Lifesight is built for consumer brands and agencies that need causal, finance-ready marketing decisions: DTC and ecommerce, omnichannel retail and CPG, and consumer apps and subscriptions. You don't need a data science team to get started. Data OS handles ingestion, transformation, and orchestration, and agents make the results accessible to everyone from the CMO to channel owners.

## Why do I need it?

No single measurement method has all the answers. MMM shows long-term, cross-channel effects but needs experimental calibration. Geo tests prove causal lift but answer one question at a time. Attribution gives daily granularity but over-credits lower-funnel channels. Lifesight triangulates all three so each fills the others' gaps, giving you incrementality you can prove to finance and the board, and spend decisions you can act on with confidence.

## How does it work?

Lifesight works in one continuous loop:

- **Data OS** connects your marketing, business, and customer data and gets it model-ready in days, not weeks.
- **The Causal AI layer** models incremental contribution with causal MMM, proves lift with geo incrementality tests, and calibrates attribution with both, supported by AI-guided model assistance.
- **The Agentic AI layer**, built on the Lifesight Agent Harness and Marketing Context Graph, turns that measurement into decisions through the cockpit, ambient cues, and artifacts.

## Why can't I just use my current attribution stack?

Most attribution tools rely on user-level tracking and correlation-based rules. Signal loss makes them less complete, and correlation doesn't tell you what your spend caused. Lifesight keeps the daily granularity of attribution but calibrates it with MMM and test-measured lift, so your daily reads reflect incrementality.

## Why are Multi-Touch Attribution platforms not accurate?

MTA assigns credit using rules or correlations across tracked touchpoints. It can't see untracked or offline exposure, and it can't separate conversions that would have happened anyway from those marketing caused. Lifesight's causal attribution calibrates touch-level credit with MMM and experimental lift, correcting over-attribution in channels like retargeting and brand search.

## What is Marketing Mix Modeling?

Marketing Mix Modeling (MMM) is an econometric technique that quantifies the incremental impact of marketing and non-marketing drivers on sales, and forecasts the impact of future budget choices. Lifesight's causal MMM measures incremental contribution, iROAS, mROAS, and saturation by channel and tactic to guide budget allocation.

## How does MMM work?

MMM analyzes historical spend and outcome data alongside drivers such as price, promos, seasonality, and macro factors, then isolates each channel's incremental effect. Lifesight's causal MMM also models adstock and lag, saturation and diminishing returns, and base sales, with adaptive baselines and regime detection (automatically spotting when your business has shifted into a new pattern) and built-in mediation, interaction, and cross-dimensional planning.

## How do I know that my MMM is accurate?

Lifesight validates models statistically and calibrates them with Experiments. Measured lift from geo tests feeds back into the model, and regular refreshes with drift monitoring keep it accurate as market conditions and consumer behavior change.

## What is ROAS?

ROAS (Return on Ad Spend) measures revenue generated per dollar spent on advertising, calculated as revenue divided by ad spend. Platform-reported ROAS often includes conversions that would have happened anyway, which is why Lifesight focuses on iROAS.

## What is Marginal ROAS or mROAS?

[Marginal ROAS](https://docs.lifesight.io/docs/insights#contribution-breakdown) (mROAS) measures the additional revenue generated by the next dollar spent on a channel. It shows where more budget will still pay off and where a channel is approaching saturation.

## What is Incrementality or iROAS?

Incrementality is the additional sales or conversions that wouldn't have happened without a specific marketing effort. [Incremental Return on Ad Spend (iROAS)](https://docs.lifesight.io/docs/insights#contribution-breakdown) expresses that as incremental revenue per dollar spent.

## What is Incrementality Testing?

[Incrementality testing](https://docs.lifesight.io/docs/why-experiment) uses controlled experiments to measure the incremental impact of a channel, campaign, or tactic, separating conversions marketing caused from those that would have happened organically.

## What is a Profit Curve or Diminishing Returns Curve?

A Profit Curve or [Diminishing Returns Curve](https://docs.lifesight.io/docs/model#saturation-and-ad-stock-curves) is a response curve showing how returns grow at a decreasing rate as spend increases in a channel. It shows the point where additional spend delivers minimal incremental gain.

## What is geo testing?

[Geo testing](https://docs.lifesight.io/docs/geo-testing) splits geographic markets into test and control groups. By comparing performance between matched markets, it measures the causal lift of a marketing change in a real-world environment.

# Experiments: Prove Incremental Lift Before You Scale

Experiments are how Lifesight turns model estimates into measured proof, and how it keeps your MMM and attribution calibrated.

## What are Experiments?

Experiments are controlled tests of marketing hypotheses, such as:

- What is the incremental impact of my current media investment on business KPIs?
- How much can I scale before hitting diminishing returns?
- Which ad creative delivers the highest incremental return?

They compare outcomes with and without a marketing activity to measure its incremental impact.

## What do Experiment results help with?

- Isolate a channel's incremental lift from external factors
- Compare channels fairly based on measured incrementality
- Allocate budget based on incremental lift
- Avoid mistaking correlation for causation
- Calibrate MMM models so they're causally grounded

## Limitations of Experiments

Experiments require time, resources, and budget. They are best suited to measuring short-term lift and are less suited to long-term effects, which is where MMM complements them. External factors can also affect results if the test isn't designed to control for them.

## How long should I run Experiments for?

Duration depends on the lift you want to detect (your MDE), your spend level, and your purchase cycle. Most geo tests run for several weeks so they capture at least one full purchase cycle and reach sufficient power. Lifesight recommends test duration as part of every test plan.

## Why should I run Experiments?

Experiments are the most direct way to prove what your marketing causes. They validate platform claims, de-risk new channels such as CTV, retail media, and influencer, settle internal debates, and calibrate your MMM and attribution so every downstream decision is grounded in measured lift.

## How do external factors impact the results of a geo test?

Local events, weather, promotions, or competitor activity can affect test and control markets differently and distort results. Lifesight reduces this risk with robust market matching, synthetic control, pre-period validation, and spillover controls, so the measured lift reflects your marketing change rather than outside noise.

## Why is it important to run split tests with sufficient traffic?

Without enough traffic, a test can't reliably tell a real effect from random variation, and results may be inconclusive or misleading. Power analysis before launch tells you how much traffic and time you need to detect the lift you care about.

## How do I ensure statistical significance in a geo test?

Run power analysis before launch to set your MDE and required duration, and aim for at least 80% power. Use well-matched test and control markets, avoid major changes in either group during the test, and read results only at the planned end date. Lifesight builds these steps into every test design.

## Geo test vs. split test vs. time test

| Test type      | Advantages                                                                                                               | Disadvantages                                                                                                                                   |
| :------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Geo test**   | Measures in a real-world environment. Isolates specific markets for comparison. Limited disruption to broader campaigns. | Needs enough markets for statistical significance. Local factors such as weather or events can affect results. Higher cost and time investment. |
| **Split test** | Highly controlled environment. Easier to measure results. Can test multiple variations at once.                          | May not reflect real-world conditions. Can need significant traffic to reach significance. Insights can be hard to apply to broader campaigns.  |
| **Time test**  | Low cost and easy to implement. Minimal disruption to overall strategy. Measures performance over time.                  | Vulnerable to seasonality and holidays. May need a long duration. Harder to isolate specific variables.                                         |

# Attribution: From Platform Credit to Causal Truth

Attribution gives you daily granularity. Lifesight's causal attribution makes sure that granularity reflects incrementality.

## What is attribution?

Attribution assigns credit to marketing touchpoints for an outcome such as a conversion or purchase, showing how each channel, campaign, or touchpoint contributes to results.

## Why is attribution important?

Attribution gives teams the daily, campaign-level reads they need to optimize bids, budgets, creative, and audiences. Calibrated with incrementality, it tells you which campaigns are truly driving results, so you can focus spend where it has the most impact.

## What types of attribution models does Lifesight support?

- **First-touch:** credits the first interaction with your brand.
- **Last-touch:** credits the final interaction before conversion.
- **Linear:** splits credit equally across all touchpoints.
- **Time decay:** gives more credit to touchpoints closer to conversion.
- **Custom:** lets you define a model tailored to your business.

Any of these can be calibrated with incrementality through causal attribution.

## How is data collected for attribution?

Lifesight collects data from web, mobile, social, email, and other channels through Data OS, then links it to user actions to trace the journey and apply your chosen attribution model.

## Can I customize my attribution model?

Yes. You can define how much weight each touchpoint receives based on your business goals or customer behavior. _To upload your own attribution weights, contact our support team._

## What is Multi-Touch Attribution?

Multi-Touch Attribution (MTA) spreads credit across multiple touchpoints in the journey, rather than giving it all to a single one. It gives a fuller view of the journey but, on its own, still measures correlation rather than incrementality.

## How often is attribution data updated?

Attribution data updates in near real time, depending on the data source, so you always have current reads for day-to-day optimization.

## Can I track offline conversions?

Yes. Lifesight supports offline conversion tracking, so you can combine offline touchpoints such as in-store purchases and phone calls with online data.

## What are common challenges with attribution?

- **Cross-device tracking:** users switch devices, which can fragment the journey.
- **Data silos:** attribution depends on accurate, integrated data from multiple sources.
- **Model bias:** different models assign credit differently, and none measure incrementality on their own.

Lifesight addresses these with cross-channel and cross-device tracking, integrations through Data OS, and causal attribution that calibrates credit with measured lift.

## How can I troubleshoot discrepancies in my attribution data?

Discrepancies usually come from:

- **Data collection issues:** make sure all touchpoints and channels are tracked.
- **Inconsistent timeframes:** compare the same time periods across platforms.
- **Model differences:** compare data within the same attribution model.

If issues persist, contact Lifesight support.

## What reports are available in Lifesight attribution?

- **Channel performance:** how each channel contributes to conversions.
- **Path analysis:** the journeys and touchpoints leading to conversion.
- **Conversion tracking:** conversions attributed to campaigns and touchpoints.

Reports can be customized to your attribution model and business needs.

## What is causal attribution?

Causal attribution applies incrementality factors from MMM and Experiments to attribution reports, calibrating touch-level credit so it reflects what marketing actually caused. It deduplicates conversions across channels and corrects over-attribution in channels like retargeting and brand search, giving you daily, granular reads aligned with causal truth.

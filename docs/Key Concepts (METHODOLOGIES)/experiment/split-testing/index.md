Split testing measures incremental impact at the user level. Your audience is divided into two groups:

* **Treatment group:** sees your ads.
* **Control group:** is held out and doesn't see your ads.

Both groups are otherwise alike. So any difference in conversions between them is the lift your ads caused.

## How a split test works

1. **Assign users:** Users are randomly split into treatment and control groups.
2. **Run the test:** Ads are shown only to the treatment group for the test period.
3. **Normalize the control:** The control group is adjusted to match the size of the treatment group, so results compare fairly.
4. **Measure lift:** Conversions in both groups are compared to calculate incremental impact.
5. **Check significance:** Results are tested to confirm the lift is real and not due to chance.

## When to use split testing

* You can target and hold out audiences at the user level.
* You want a fast, direct read on a channel, campaign, or audience.
* You need clean results to calibrate your Marketing Mix Model.

When user-level tracking is limited, [Geo-Testing](https://docs.lifesight.io/update/docs/what-is-geo-testing) measures lift through regional differences instead.

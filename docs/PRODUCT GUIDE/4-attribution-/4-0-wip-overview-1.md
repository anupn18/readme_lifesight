Overview shows whether the money you spent in the selected period actually produced revenue, and whether you are on track against the plan you committed to. It brings spend, incremental revenue, and pacing into one screen so you can decide what to fund, what to cut, and what to fix before the period closes.

![](https://files.readme.io/fdd1fc0884c1895708d8a2bb5bd3274d39dbce4b88f618e601f4d3ac70e02d63-Screenshot_2026-09-18_at_3.34.05_PM.png)

Everything on this screen is scoped by two controls: the date picker, which sets the reporting range, and the target scenario control, which sets the plan you are being measured against. Change either one and every metric below recalculates.

## Start with the four numbers that tell you where you stand

The cards at the top give you the shortest possible read on performance.

- **Total Spend&#x20;**&#x73;hows what you have invested across all channels in the selected range, with the number of days elapsed in the scenario so you can judge whether the spend level is reasonable for how far into the period you are.
- **Incremental Revenue** shows the revenue Lifesight estimates you would not have earned without the advertising, along with its share of platform-reported revenue. The gap between the two is the part of platform-reported revenue that would likely have converted anyway.
- **iROAS&#x20;**(incremental return on ad spend) shows incremental revenue divided by spend, set against platform ROAS for the same period. Platform ROAS will almost always be higher. The distance between them tells you how much of your reported performance is credit the platforms are claiming for conversions they did not cause.
- **Net vs Plan&#x20;**&#x73;hows how far above or below your planned spend you are, with overall pacing for the scenario. Read this alongside elapsed days: being 10% under plan means something very different on day 3 than on day 25.

Read the cards together rather than one at a time. Rising spend with flat incremental revenue means you are buying volume that is not adding demand. Flat spend with rising iROAS usually means mix or creative has improved.

## Check pacing before you judge performance

The pacing section compares actual spend with planned spend from the active scenario, so you can separate a performance problem from a delivery problem. A channel that looks weak on revenue may simply never have received the budget it was planned for.

Here you can review:

- **Scenario progress**, or how much of the period has elapsed
- **Cumulative spend&#x20;**&#x76;ersus plan, which shows whether the gap is widening or closing over time
- **Channel counts&#x20;**&#x73;plit into on track, behind plan, and ahead of plan, so you know how concentrated the problem is

**Suggested daily adjustments&#x20;**&#x63;onvert those gaps into a number you can act on. For under-pacing channels, it shows how much daily spend needs to scale to land on plan by the end of the period. For over-pacing channels, it shows how much to ease off to avoid overshooting. These are spend corrections, not performance recommendations, so pair them with the channel table before you move budget.

## See how much of your reported revenue you actually caused

This comparison puts platform-reported revenue next to the revenue Lifesight estimates was causally incremental (revenue that would not have happened without the ad).

The **incrementality factor** is the share of platform-reported revenue estimated to be incremental. A factor of 0.4 on a channel means roughly 40 cents of every reported dollar is genuinely new revenue, and the rest is demand that was already coming.

This is the number to bring to any conversation about why your platform dashboards and your revenue do not reconcile. Channels with high reported revenue and a low incrementality factor are usually harvesting existing demand rather than creating it, which is a signal to rebalance rather than to switch off.

## Decide where the next dollar should go

The channel table ranks every channel by incremental value, so the channels contributing most real revenue sit at the top regardless of how large their reported numbers are.

For each channel you can review:

- **Spend** in the selected range
- **Incremental Revenue** attributed to that channel
- **Platform ROAS** as reported by the platform
- **Incrementality factor,** so you can see how much of that reported performance holds up
- **Pacing&#x20;**&#x61;gainst the channel's planned spend
- **Recommendation,&#x20;**&#x77;hich combines incrementality and pacing into a suggested direction such as scaling, holding, or reducing
- **Actions,&#x20;**&#x77;here you open the recommendation to see the reasoning behind it and act on it

The pattern worth looking for is a channel with strong platform ROAS, a low incrementality factor, and ahead-of-plan pacing. That is budget working hard on paper and producing little in reality, and it is usually the fastest place to free up spend for channels that are under-pacing with a high incrementality factor.

## Change the period or the benchmark you are measured against

Use the date picker to change the reporting range. Shorter ranges surface recent shifts in performance; longer ranges are more reliable for channels with slower conversion cycles, since incremental effects take time to register.

Use the target scenario control to review or change the active benchmark. The scenario is the plan your pacing, Net vs Plan, and suggested daily adjustments are all calculated against, so switching scenarios changes what counts as on track without changing any of your actual spend or revenue data.
